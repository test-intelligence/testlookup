# JR-05 — Summary Report Journey

Status: **partial.** Steps 1-2 measured and **passing**; steps 3-6 not measured.

## Step 1-2 — aggregation and metrics, independently re-derived

The plan is explicit: "re-derive independently with SQL, do not trust the page's
own second number." So the service's semantics were read first (`_window_totals`
uses `DISTINCT ON (test_fingerprint)`, taking each fingerprint's status from its
most recent execution in the window), then re-implemented as a separate query.

`GET /api/v1/reports/summary?project_id=852a9cf9…&days=365`:

```
total_test_cases 112   passed 85   failed 21   skipped 5   broken 1
evaluated        107
pass_rate_pct    75.9   weighted_pass_rate_pct 79.4
pass_rate_basis  unique_tests ("per unique test")
```

Independent SQL over the same window and semantics:

```
112|85|21|5|1|75.9|79.4
```

**Exact match on all seven values.** The API's counts and both rates are
arithmetically sound. `85+21+5+1 = 112` closes, `evaluated = 85+21+1 = 107`
closes, and the four composition rates (75.9 + 18.8 + 4.5 + 0.9) sum to 100.1 —
they are a genuine partition over `total`, which is *why* `pass_rate_pct` divides
by `total` rather than `evaluated`.

The plan's own spec was stale here: it lists `GET /api/v1/summary-report`, but the
router prefix is `/api/v1/reports/summary`. The former returns 404.

## Observation — recorded, not filed as a defect

The headline KPI is `totals.pass_rate_pct` = `passed / total`, i.e. **skipped
tests included in the denominator**, while the canonical run-level rule
(`ingestion.py:952`, and the rate on `/runs`) excludes them. For this project
that is 75.9% against 79.4% — a **3.5 percentage point** gap under one word,
"Pass %".

Why this was not filed:

- Both numbers are published, and the tile's subtitle shows the weighted one.
- The four composition rates only sum to 100% because they share the `total`
  denominator; moving the headline to `evaluated` would break that partition.
- `summary_report_service.py:221-227` carries an explicit instruction against it:
  "Do not fix this by switching the basis without deciding that first."

What is genuinely weak is the **label**. `PASS_RATE_BASIS_LABELS` offers "per test
execution" and "per unique test" — both describe the *population*, neither
mentions skips. A reader comparing `/reports/summary` with `/runs` for the same
data sees two numbers and no explanation. A labelling change is cheap and needs
no basis change; which number should headline the page is the owner's call.
