## Reproduction

1. Open `/releases` as QA_LEAD or ADMIN.
2. Click any of the four controls above.

Expected: the control performs its action, or is visibly disabled with a reason.

Observed: a toast reading `<action> — coming in next iteration`. The control is
styled and positioned exactly like the working controls beside it.

## Evidence

```
frontend/src/components/releases/ReleaseCard.tsx:99  toast('Clone from previous release — coming in next iteration')
frontend/src/components/releases/ReleaseCard.tsx:106 toast('Generate from PRD — coming in next iteration')
frontend/src/pages/ReleasesPage.tsx:819              toast('Export schedule — coming in next iteration')
frontend/src/pages/ReleasesPage.tsx:826              toast('Calendar view — coming in next iteration')
```

Count verified by grep across `frontend/src`, excluding tests: **four**, not two.
A 2026-09-05 design handoff recorded this item as "two placeholder buttons on
`ReleaseCard.tsx`" and set an acceptance criterion of "no button in the app
toasts 'coming in next iteration'". As scoped, that item could not have met its
own criterion — the two on `ReleasesPage.tsx` were never in it.

## Why this is not fixed in this program

Removing or disabling a control the product intends to ship is a product
decision, not a defect fix. There are three defensible outcomes and they differ
in user-visible behaviour:

1. Implement the four actions.
2. Remove the controls until the actions exist.
3. Keep them, visibly disabled, with a tooltip naming what is missing.

This program does not choose between those unilaterally. Filed with the count
corrected so whoever does decide is working from four, not two.

## Tests

None added. A regression test here would pin whichever behaviour is chosen, and
that choice has not been made. When it is, the test belongs with it.

## Recommended acceptance check for whoever takes it

`grep -rn "coming in next iteration" frontend/src --include=*.tsx --include=*.ts`
returns nothing outside tests, and each of the four controls either acts or is
disabled with a reason a user can read.
